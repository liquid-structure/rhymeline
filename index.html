For each channel, this checks every lag x: out of all pairs of moments x steps apart, what share land within the tolerance of each other — versus the baseline rate for that channel overall. A bar well above the baseline line means that spacing repeats more than chance alone would produce. With many lags tested, some bars will clear the bar by luck — treat a single spike with caution, especially on short series, and more so as ± tolerance and channel count grow.

HIGH-PROBABILITY REPEAT EVENTS
Next-step values come from the analog forecast above: the median outcome across matched historical moments. Repeat events come from the dimension-rhythm analysis: a specific number, channel, and future spacing where that channel's own history repeats more than chance alone would. Both are empirical estimates from a limited sample, not guarantees — read the probability as relative confidence, not certainty, and treat any "low confidence" event as a hint rather than a call.

This is pattern-similarity across channels, not causality. A short, noisy series will produce a small analog sample and a wide, low-confidence band — that width is information too, not a flaw to fix.
